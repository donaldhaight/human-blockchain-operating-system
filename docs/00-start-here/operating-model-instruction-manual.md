# Living-Program Instruction Manual

## Preface

This manual establishes the common production standard for the **Human Blockchain Operating System** and its agent-company planning track. It exists so that each future answer, requirement, design, software artifact, business plan, publication, or media concept can be created from a shared structure rather than from isolated conversations.

The project remains human-governed. Donald Haight is the final authority for business direction, commitments, capital, legal posture, sensitive data, public representation, and production release. Agents may research, structure, draft, build, test, and maintain records within defined authority; they do not silently convert a suggestion into a binding action. This follows the project authority order and safety boundaries already recorded in the continuity brief and registers. [1] [2] [3]

## 1. Purpose and Operating Rule

Every substantial artifact must make its purpose, intended audience, authority status, source basis, limitations, decision impact, and next action explicit. A document is not automatically an approved decision merely because it is written clearly. The record must distinguish **historical source**, **working draft**, **proposed decision**, **accepted decision**, **tested result**, and **external commitment**.

The default operating rule is:

> **Capture the change in the right record before treating it as current operating truth.**

A new insight becomes a requirement, decision, assumption, risk, question, backlog item, design artifact, or research task according to its actual role. It must not remain only in chat memory.

## 2. Required Elements for Significant Outputs

| Element | Default requirement | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Specific, audience-readable title | All significant artifacts |
| Tagline | One concise statement of purpose or promise | Plans, public materials, media concepts |
| Blurb | One-paragraph explanation for a new reader | Plans, proposals, case studies, releases |
| Executive summary | Decision-relevant summary before detail | Business, product, technical, research outputs |
| Audience | State who should use, approve, or review the artifact | All significant artifacts |
| Scope | State what is included and deliberately excluded | Plans, requirements, technical work |
| Authority status | Identify draft, proposal, accepted decision, or review-only status | All significant artifacts |
| Body | Present conclusion first, then supporting reasoning and implementation detail | All written work |
| Evidence and sources | Cite external facts and link relevant internal records | Research, business, technical, public work |
| Requirements and tests | Include acceptance criteria where the artifact directs implementation | Product, design, engineering work |
| Risks and gates | Identify material uncertainty, legal sensitivity, privacy, security, or dependency | Plans and consequential workflows |
| Closing | State the immediate decision, open question, or next controlled action | All significant artifacts |
| References | Use numeric references and a complete reference list | Research-grounded work |
| Change record | Preserve revision date, owner, and related records | Living plans and specifications |

## 3. Source, Image, and Rights Practices

Source material must remain distinguishable from derived interpretation. Preserve original files unchanged when possible, identify provenance, and make a claim's evidentiary basis visible. Historical content is a source of requirements and alternatives; it does not override current founder narration or approved decisions.

Images are used only when they improve understanding, decision quality, accessibility, or emotional communication. Each image must have descriptive alt text, a source or generation record, and a documented usage right or permission basis. Product specifications should use diagrams only when they clarify actors, states, interfaces, or evidence flow. Marketing, books, films, music videos, and public sites may use richer visual systems, but visuals must not imply unverified performance, authority, endorsement, financial return, legal outcome, insurance coverage, or product capability.

Do not use customer, claim, medical, financial, legal, identity, or location-sensitive imagery without an approved privacy and rights basis. Synthetic demonstration assets must be labeled as synthetic where a reasonable viewer could otherwise mistake them for a real operating result.

## 4. Structures for Written, Product, and Media Artifacts

### Business Plan, Operating Plan, or Investor-Ready Narrative

Use a title, tagline, blurb, executive summary, problem, solution, operating model, target market, customer, business model, differentiation, evidence, staged roadmap, team and authority model, financial assumptions, risks, dependencies, request or decision, and references. Clearly label estimates, scenarios, historical facts, and accepted commitments differently.

### Product Requirements Document and Software Specification

Use a product summary, user and stakeholder map, problem statement, scope, exclusions, functional requirements, non-functional requirements, data model impact, permissions, workflow or state model, integration boundaries, acceptance criteria, quality and security tests, dependencies, launch gates, and open questions. A requirement is complete only when a builder and tester can determine whether it is satisfied.

### Case Study, White Paper, Research Paper, or Explainer

Use an abstract or summary, introduction, question or thesis, context, evidence base, analysis, alternative interpretations, limitations, implications, conclusion, and references. A case study may include a chronological narrative, but it must not present future claims as observed results.

### Website, Application, or Dashboard

Use a product statement, intended audience, role and permission boundary, user journeys, information architecture, screen inventory, accessibility requirements, data handling rules, service integration boundaries, analytics plan, error and recovery behavior, acceptance tests, deployment plan, and operating ownership. Each consequential action must identify the actor, authority, object, evidence requirement, and review path.

### Slide Deck or Presentation

Use a title slide, audience promise, concise problem framing, thesis, supporting evidence, visual explanation, operating or product model, decision or request, risks, appendix, and sources. Slides are a presentation layer, not the sole source of truth for requirements or legal commitments.

### Book, Memoir, Fiction, or Narrative Architecture

Use a title, subtitle, author note or preface, blurb, reader promise, table of contents, story or argument arc, chapter plan, voice and point-of-view rules, source and rights notes where relevant, acknowledgments, closing material, and bibliography when factual claims are made. Creative treatment must preserve the difference between fictionalized narrative and verified history.

### Music Video, Film, or Motion Narrative

Use title, logline, treatment, creative brief, target audience, runtime, song or script rights, visual world, chapter or scene manifest, character/performer treatment, shot plan, production design, image and footage provenance, accessibility captions, credits, technical master specifications, distribution plan, and final approval gate. No audio, likeness, trademark, or third-party footage may be used without a sufficient rights basis.

### Marketing Campaign, Public Announcement, or Sales Material

Use audience, campaign objective, offer, evidence-supported claim set, disclosures, channel plan, creative assets, conversion measurement, approval owner, publication schedule, and correction or takedown process. Public claims require the evidence and authorized-disclosure gate identified in the risk register. [3]

## 5. Living-Plan Maintenance Protocol

The **business plan**, **requirements register**, **decision log**, **assumption register**, **risk register**, **question register**, **roadmap**, and **backlog** are linked views of one operating model. Each new conversation item is classified as follows.

| New information | Record to update | Minimum contents |
|---|---|---|
| Founder direction | Decision register and affected plan | Decision, status, authority, reason, consequences |
| Feature or capability | Requirements and backlog | User value, priority, acceptance criteria, dependencies |
| Unverified belief or estimate | Assumptions and variables register | Assumption, validation method, owner, review date |
| Potential downside | Risks and review gates | Risk, impact, control, named approval gate |
| Unresolved choice | Open-question register | Question, priority, decision owner, decision path |
| Observed result | Case-study or operational evidence | Context, measurement basis, limitation, implication |
| Architecture change | Technical plan, decision record, tests | Boundary, rationale, migration impact, validation |

No requirement becomes a production action merely because an agent proposes it. When the item changes authority, money, legal posture, sensitive data, public exposure, ownership, access, or irreversible records, it must pass the relevant human approval gate.

## 6. Agent Authority and Quality Controls

An agent may work only from a defined objective, bounded context, stated assumptions, and acceptance criteria. Its output must identify source lineage, uncertainty, test results, changed files or records, and escalation needs. Every operational agent must have a named human accountable for its work domain.

Agents may prepare research, specifications, drafts, code, tests, documentation, task suggestions, and internal analyses. Agents may not independently bind an entity, make a legal or insurance determination, move money, grant production access, accept an offer, publish a material claim, submit an official record, or alter ownership or security controls. Any future delegation must be narrow, auditable, revocable, and lawful.

Quality uses a dual control model. First, the producing agent tests against stated acceptance criteria. Second, an independent reviewer, test suite, or accountable human checks material outputs. The system preserves evidence of both the action and the review rather than relying on a model's self-report.

## 7. Conversation Protocol

Questions may be asked continuously in ordinary language. The response should remain brief and direct unless deeper analysis is requested. Behind the response, the relevant living record is updated when the exchange creates a requirement, decision, question, assumption, risk, or changed priority.

For each substantive question, the maintainer will state: the short answer; the affected planning area, if any; and the one most useful next question when a founder decision is needed. The conversation is not a substitute for a decision register, but it is the input channel that keeps the register alive.

## Closing

This manual creates a repeatable way to translate founder intent into controlled work without flattening the system or giving agents unbounded authority. It is deliberately broad enough to govern business plans, software specifications, research, websites, media, and future operating artifacts while keeping each output specific to its intended use.
