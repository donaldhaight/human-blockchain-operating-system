# Legal Services and Jurisdictional Attorney Network Operating System

> **Tagline:** A human-governed system for coordinating jurisdiction-qualified legal services around the InsurTech ecosystem without disguising the practice of law, weakening independent counsel, or bypassing professional and insurance regulation.

## Executive Summary

The requested function is feasible only if it is built as a **lawful legal-services and jurisdictional-attorney network**, not as a “front.” A DAO, technology company, platform, cooperative, insurer, reciprocal attorney-in-fact, or operating company cannot become attorney of record merely by designation, ownership, software, or governance. For a particular matter, the attorney of record must be the appropriately admitted and authorized lawyer or law firm, retained through a valid engagement and acting under the rules applicable to that jurisdiction and forum.

The operating system can coordinate a network of independently qualified attorneys, maintain jurisdictional coverage records, organize legal requests, support conflict checks, prepare matter materials, safeguard access, and create an auditable review workflow. It can also help the InsurTech and any future reciprocal-insurance initiative identify when counsel, insurance-regulatory review, or a state-specific pathway is needed. It cannot direct legal judgment, control a lawyer's professional independence, decide whether a client is represented, share fees improperly, make legal claims through agents, or use a DAO as a device to evade professional-responsibility or insurance rules. [1]

A reciprocal insurance exchange's **attorney-in-fact** should be treated as an insurance-management concept, not as interchangeable with attorney-at-law or attorney of record. Any reciprocal, attorney-in-fact, legal-services, or jurisdictional-launch structure must be examined state by state by qualified insurance, professional-responsibility, corporate, tax, privacy, and employment counsel before it is formed, marketed, funded, or used.

## 1. Purpose and Non-Evasion Rule

This operating system exists to make lawful representation and legal review easier to coordinate—not to create the appearance of representation where it does not exist. It translates the project’s existing requirement that legal, dispute, and advocacy services occur through a separate engagement with a licensed professional or entity, with no hidden platform representation. [1]

> **Non-evasion rule:** No platform, DAO, operating company, reciprocal attorney-in-fact, agent, or administrative entity may be presented as an attorney of record, law firm, legal representative, or legal decision maker unless the applicable jurisdiction permits that exact role and an appropriately authorized legal professional/entity has accepted the representation.

The program must use precise language. “Attorney network,” “counsel coordination,” “jurisdictional coverage record,” “legal-service referral or intake,” “professional review,” and “separate engagement” may describe limited functions when true. “Licensed in a county,” “attorney of record,” “legal representation,” “legal approval,” “legal clearance,” and “legal DAO” require jurisdiction-specific confirmation and must not be used as marketing shorthand.

## 2. Legal-Service, Insurance, and DAO Layers

The architecture separates four layers. Each must maintain its own authority, records, terms, conflicts, and financial treatment.

| Layer | Permitted purpose | Must remain separate from |
|---|---|---|
| InsurTech platform | User-directed workflow, evidence, document organization, task routing, and external-determination recording | Legal advice, representation, insurance determination, claim advocacy, custody, and unapproved regulated activity |
| Legal-services professional layer | Actual legal advice and representation through appropriately qualified lawyers/law firms | Platform operational control, nonlawyer direction of legal judgment, unapproved fee arrangements, and implied representation |
| Insurance / reciprocal layer | Any authorized insurance, reciprocal, attorney-in-fact, claims, or policyholder function | Attorney-at-law status, legal representation, and unauthorized cross-role decision making |
| DAO or governance layer | Limited governance, standards, member coordination, audit, and non-legal resource allocation where lawful | Practice of law, client representation, legal supervision, attorney-of-record status, trust-money control, and workarounds for professional rules |

The existence of a shared mission, entity, brand, or technology stack does not merge these layers. A member attorney's professional duty is to their client, not to the platform, DAO, insurer, sponsor, contractor, or founder.

## 3. Viable Operating Structures

The founder should select a structure only after jurisdictional professional and insurance review. These options are alternatives, not an automatic sequence.

| Approach | Tradeoffs | Cost | Setup complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent attorney network with separate client engagements | Fastest path to counsel coverage and preserves lawyer independence; the platform provides intake and coordination only. It does not own representation or control advice. | Lower initial operating cost; each firm bears its own professional overhead | Moderate; requires attorney verification, conflicts, terms, privacy controls, and clear routing |
| Lawyer-controlled professional entity plus separate technology services company | Creates a dedicated legal-services brand and operational process when permitted; requires strict professional ownership/control, ethics, fee, confidentiality, and corporate-practice analysis in each applicable jurisdiction. | Moderate to high; entity, insurance, counsel, procedures, and compliance costs | High; jurisdiction-specific structural review required before launch |
| DAO-supported professional network | May provide member governance, standards, education, shared non-legal tools, and transparent internal coordination where lawful; it cannot become a shell for a nonlawyer-controlled practice or client representation. | Variable; governance, audit, and technology burden can become material | High; requires professional-responsibility, entity, securities, tax, data, and insurance review |
| Future reciprocal-insurance initiative with separate attorney-in-fact and independent legal counsel | May be relevant to a future reciprocal-insurance model; the attorney-in-fact's insurance-management role must not be mislabeled as a legal-services or attorney-of-record function. | Highest; formation, capitalization, regulatory approvals, service providers, and governance are substantial | Highest; a distinct insurance venture requiring separate professional planning |

The initial recommendation is the **independent attorney network with separate client engagements**. It produces the clearest evidence about demand and workflow while avoiding premature structure, ownership, or representation claims.

## 4. Jurisdictional Coverage Model

Attorneys are generally authorized by state, court, or other relevant professional admission—not by a commercial “license” granted by the platform for counties. The system may maintain a **Jurisdictional Counsel Coverage Record** that identifies where a lawyer is independently eligible to practice, what types of matters the lawyer has agreed to consider, which courts or venues are relevant, and whether any local counsel or admission process may be required. Each entry requires verification and review; it is not a platform credential or a representation guarantee.

| Coverage field | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Attorney / firm identity | Verified identity, professional entity, primary contact, and current affiliation record |
| Jurisdiction basis | State bar, court admission, pro hac vice pathway, local-counsel requirement, or other stated authorization basis; verify against an appropriate primary source where available |
| County / venue coverage | Availability, practice focus, venue familiarity, travel/service capability, or local-counsel relationship; never label this a legal license without a verified legal basis |
| Matter scope | Limited intake categories and explicit exclusions; not an assertion that representation will be accepted |
| Insurance and reciprocal capability | Separate identification of relevant insurance-regulatory, coverage, claims, corporate, or litigation experience; not a substitute for admission or engagement |
| Conflict and capacity | Current conflict-screen and capacity status before any referral, consultation, or representation claim |
| Engagement status | No relationship / consultation pending / separate engagement executed / declined / concluded; do not infer representation from a directory entry |
| Verification and expiration | Verification source/date, renewal reminder, review owner, and removal/suspension process |

## 5. Matter Lifecycle and Attorney-of-Record Controls

A legal matter can enter the system only through a controlled request and reach attorney-of-record status only through the lawyer/law firm's own compliant process. The platform must not declare or imply that representation begins earlier.

| Stage | System function | Required evidence | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 — General information | Provide educational content and self-help platform guidance | Content version and disclaimers | Personalized legal advice or claim strategy by an agent/platform |
| L1 — Intake request | Collect authorized intake information with appropriate notice and security | Request record, user notice, data classification, selected jurisdiction | Promising representation, outcome, or attorney availability |
| L2 — Routing candidate | Identify a potential independently qualified lawyer/firm based on allowed criteria | Coverage record, conflict/capacity request, routing logic, user notice | Treating a directory match as an engagement or charging unreviewed referral compensation |
| L3 — Conflict and independent decision | Lawyer/firm conducts conflicts and determines whether to proceed | Lawyer-controlled record or affirmative result, where shareable | Platform deciding a conflict or controlling counsel's acceptance |
| L4 — Separate engagement | Client and lawyer/law firm execute the applicable agreement | Engagement status, scope, client/lawyer identity, effective date; minimize platform access | Platform signing or accepting on behalf of either party |
| L5 — Matter support | Platform provides only expressly authorized technology/administrative support | Access authorization, task scope, audit record | Agent/platform directing legal judgment, filing, negotiation, trust funds, or client communication as counsel |
| L6 — Attorney-of-record action | Counsel enters appearance or serves in a formal role if authorized by relevant forum and engagement | Lawyer/firm-controlled record and applicable filing/appearance evidence | Calling the platform, DAO, reciprocal, or agent attorney of record |
| L7 — Closeout and retention | Counsel closes representation; platform handles only permitted record retention and access | Closeout status, retention/disposition rule, access revocation | Retaining or repurposing privileged/controlled material without authority |

## 6. Minimum Requirements

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| LNOS-001 | State the legal-services product boundary in every pilot and public material | P0 | The product describes coordination/intake/technology only until a separate engagement exists |
| LNOS-002 | Maintain the four-layer separation model | P0 | Legal, InsurTech, insurance/reciprocal, and DAO records/permissions are separate and auditable |
| LNOS-003 | Maintain a verified Jurisdictional Counsel Coverage Record | P0 | Each active attorney/firm record has identity, jurisdiction basis, scope, verification date, owner, and expiration/review state |
| LNOS-004 | Require lawyer-controlled conflict and capacity review | P0 | The platform can request routing but cannot decide conflicts or represent acceptance of a matter |
| LNOS-005 | Require independent client engagement before representation status | P0 | The platform cannot mark a client represented or an attorney of record without evidence from the authorized lawyer/law firm workflow |
| LNOS-006 | Block agents and nonlegal staff from legal advice and representation actions | P0 | Protected-action tests block legal conclusion, strategy, filing, negotiation, engagement, appearance, and attorney-of-record declarations |
| LNOS-007 | Control confidentiality and privilege-sensitive data | P0 | Matter data has a sensitivity class, need-to-know access, audit trail, approved processor/access path, retention rule, and export/revocation process |
| LNOS-008 | Separate fee, referral, and trust-money treatment | P0 | No fee split, referral compensation, trust-account, retainer, or client-fund workflow activates without jurisdiction-specific professional and financial review |
| LNOS-009 | Preserve client choice and informed routing disclosures | P0 | User receives clear notice that routing is not representation, may choose other counsel, and knows what information will be shared |
| LNOS-010 | Maintain a professional-independence control | P0 | No platform/DAO/insurer/contractor actor can direct case strategy, legal judgment, client acceptance, filing, settlement, or withdrawal |
| LNOS-011 | Maintain attorney-of-record evidence | P0 | Any attorney-of-record designation identifies the actual lawyer/firm, matter, forum, authority, effective status, and supporting record |
| LNOS-012 | Keep reciprocal attorney-in-fact records distinct from attorney-at-law records | P0 | UI, data model, and communications cannot conflate insurance-management authority with legal representation |
| LNOS-013 | Implement ethics, advertising, and communications review | P1 | Public and targeted legal-service materials have a named reviewer, jurisdiction scope, source basis, and correction/withdrawal path |
| LNOS-014 | Support counsel-approved administrative workflows | P1 | Task scope, access authorization, human owner, and prohibition against legal judgment are recorded for each support function |
| LNOS-015 | Maintain complaint, incident, and withdrawal/decline handling | P1 | A matter can be held, reassigned, denied, or closed with appropriate records and access controls |
| LNOS-016 | Use a legal-matter launch gate | P1 | Each real client, firm, jurisdictional expansion, or material integration has completed and accountable go/hold/no-go evidence |
| LNOS-017 | Maintain jurisdiction-specific policy versions | P1 | Relevant notices, routing rules, and professional rules are versioned by jurisdiction and review date |
| LNOS-018 | Preserve data and decision provenance | P2 | A reviewer can distinguish user input, attorney work product, platform record, agent draft, and external source without flattening their authority |

## 7. Agent and Platform Boundaries

Agents may organize intake, extract authorized metadata, locate counsel-coverage candidates from verified records, identify missing documents, prepare non-legal summaries, generate administrative drafts for attorney review, and maintain audit-ready work records. All agent work must be treated as assistance, not legal advice or a substitute for counsel judgment.

| Protected action | Required control |
|---|---|
| Give legal advice, interpret law for a user, or recommend a legal strategy | Block; route to qualified counsel through a separate engagement process |
| Form an attorney-client relationship or accept representation | Block; only the lawyer/law firm may make the decision through its own process |
| Declare or file an attorney-of-record appearance | Block; require actual lawyer/firm and forum-specific authorized workflow |
| Conduct conflict analysis as the final decision | Assist only; final review belongs to the lawyer/law firm |
| Negotiate, settle, sign, or file on a client's behalf | Block absent lawyer-controlled, jurisdictionally lawful workflow and explicit client authority |
| Control trust funds, retainers, legal fees, or fee allocation | Block absent a separately approved professional/financial structure |
| Share privileged, confidential, or sensitive matter data | Enforce need-to-know, authorization, audit, and approved-processor controls |
| Use a DAO vote or token mechanism to decide a client's legal matter | Block; professional judgment and client relationship cannot be delegated to platform governance |

## 8. Attorney Network, Reciprocal, and Insurance Interfaces

The attorney network can support an insurance-adjacent ecosystem only through explicit interfaces. It must not become the hidden decision engine for coverage, claims, payment, or reciprocal governance.

| Interface | Permitted planning function | Required gate before activation |
|---|---|---|
| InsurTech to attorney network | User-requested intake/routing, legal-readiness issue identification, and authorized matter support | Data sharing basis, conflict/capacity process, client-choice notice, separate engagement, privacy/security review |
| Attorney network to insured/user | Legal services only when independently accepted by qualified counsel | Engagement, jurisdiction/admission confirmation, professional-independence and communications controls |
| Attorney network to contractor | General education or separate counsel relationship as appropriate | Conflict analysis, scope definition, no unauthorized claim advocacy, appropriate disclosures |
| Attorney network to carrier/reciprocal | Regulatory, coverage, corporate, litigation, or transaction counsel when retained by the relevant entity | Separate client/entity identification, engagement, conflict review, authority, confidentiality, and insurance-regulatory review |
| Reciprocal attorney-in-fact to legal counsel | Seek advice/representation as a distinct insurance-management client | Separate identity, authority, engagement, and no-conflation control |
| DAO/governance to counsel | Request policy or governance review as a client only if an authorized legal entity retains counsel | Clear client identity, authorized representative, engagement, privilege/access boundary |

## 9. Launch Gates

The completed [Legal Matter and Jurisdictional Launch-Gate Template](./legal-matter-jurisdictional-launch-gate-template.md) is required before opening a real client intake, publishing a legal-service offering, adding a jurisdictional coverage claim, activating a lawyer/firms' platform access, or representing any attorney as counsel/attorney of record.

| Gate | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| Jurisdiction | Named state/forum/county or venue context, applicable rule-research owner, and expiration/review date |
| Professional structure | Approved model for attorney/firm independence, entity relationship, administrative support, compensation, and marketing |
| Attorney verification | Identity, admission/authorization record, scope, coverage record, conflict/capacity path, and renewal review |
| Client relationship | User notice, informed choice, authorized data sharing, and separate engagement requirement |
| Data and privilege | Classification, access boundaries, processor review, incident response, retention, and withdrawal/offboarding procedure |
| Platform and agent controls | Tested blocks for advice, representation, attorney-of-record, filing, negotiation, trust funds, and legal-decision functions |
| Insurance/reciprocal separation | Clear client/entity and authority records; attorney-in-fact label separated from attorney-at-law/record functions |
| Communications | Reviewed claims, disclaimers, advertising, referrals, and escalation/contact path |
| Retrospective | Actual result, complaint/incident record, professional feedback, and required plan/register updates |

## 10. Staged Roadmap

### Stage L0 — Map the boundaries before recruiting counsel

Define the initial legal-service product posture, target jurisdictions, attorney and client relationship, initial matter scope, data sensitivity, and prohibited functions. Build the issue queue for professional-responsibility, corporate-practice, referral/fee, advertising, privilege, insurance, reciprocal, privacy, and entity questions. This stage creates no legal-service offering.

### Stage L1 — Build an internal legal-readiness system

Implement the Jurisdictional Counsel Coverage Record, legal issue queue, professional-review workflow, data controls, and routing disclosures. Use synthetic or internal scenarios only. Prove that agents cannot give advice or declare representation, and that the platform preserves the distinction among an attorney, attorney-in-fact, Claim Caller, and platform user.

### Stage L2 — Conduct limited counsel-network discovery

With approved, non-misleading materials, identify independently qualified lawyers/firms willing to consider a network relationship. Do not promise referrals, representation, revenue, or client volume. Each potential counsel relationship requires a professional-independence, conflicts, fee, data, and jurisdiction review before activation.

### Stage L3 — Run a controlled jurisdictional pilot

Open only the reviewed workflow in one jurisdiction under a launch-gate record. The first pilot should emphasize legal-readiness and authorized intake/routing, not automated legal service. Capture actual outcomes, client/attorney experience, data-access issues, escalation quality, and any no-go facts.

### Stage L4 — Add jurisdictions and insurance interfaces deliberately

Add one jurisdiction or interface at a time. Update the coverage record, policy versions, professional terms, and data controls. Evaluate each carrier, reciprocal, attorney-in-fact, contractor, or InsurTech interface as a separate authority and conflict question.

### Stage L5 — Evaluate an authorized professional-entity or reciprocal pathway

Only after recurring evidence supports it should the founder evaluate a lawyer-controlled professional entity, more formal network, DAO governance structure, or reciprocal-insurance initiative. Each is a distinct legal and operating venture requiring counsel and potentially regulators; none is created by this operating-system plan.

## 11. Founder Decisions Required Before Implementation

1. **Initial product posture:** Should the first version remain internal legal-readiness and counsel-coordination infrastructure, or should it proceed toward a limited external attorney-network pilot after professional review?
2. **Initial jurisdiction:** Which state and forum/venue context should govern the first coverage record and policy review?
3. **First legal client:** Who is the intended client for any initial legal work: an individual property owner, RRCA, USA LLC, a prospective reciprocal/attorney-in-fact entity, a contractor, or another defined entity? One matter must not assume all are the same client.
4. **Attorney-network model:** Is the initial target an independent lawyer/firm referral/coordination network, a later lawyer-controlled entity, or a DAO-supported standards/community layer only?
5. **Insurance-reciprocal scope:** Is a reciprocal insurance exchange an exploratory future venture, or is there an active intent to form one? If active, who owns insurance-regulatory counsel coordination and the initial state analysis?
6. **Data posture:** Should the legal-readiness proof use synthetic/internal scenarios only, or can it use real intake after appropriate notices, engagement, access controls, and review exist?

## Closing

The lawful system is not a legal front. It is a **transparent attorney-network and legal-services coordination operating system** that respects client choice, counsel independence, jurisdictional authorization, confidentiality, and the separation between technology, insurance operations, governance, and legal representation.

Its first accomplishment should be modest but real: establish a verified jurisdictional coverage record, an auditable intake-to-separate-engagement workflow, agent blocks against unauthorized legal actions, and a single-jurisdiction legal-readiness pilot. Only evidence from that process should inform any later professional-entity, DAO-governance, reciprocal-insurance, or multi-state expansion decision.
